Milestone 4 will comprise about 6% of your overall course grade.

## Goal

In the final project milestone, there are three goals:

1. Share your visualizations in Lab with classmates informally, get feedback, and implement it.

2. Polish and add finishing touches to your visualizations, make sure you meet all the requirements, add add a final reflection (individually on PrairieLearn).

3. Present your dashboard live in class, and then record a high-quality demo reel of your project for final grading.

## 3. Present Dashboard in class and Demo Reel

There are two parts to this component, a dashboard presentation in class, as well as a demo reel.

### Present Dashboard in class

Each group will choose a ten-minute timeslot during the last two classes of the term.
In that time slot, each group member will present *one* of their views in a short 2-minute presentation.
Suggested guidelines for the presentation is given below.
Following the presentations, the group will answer questions from the audience and receive additional feedback for ~ 2 minutes.
While the group is taking questions, the next group will come down to the podium and setup.

#### Guidelines on two-minute presentations

The presentation must include either a live-demo of the visualization (recommended!) or if necessary, a pre-recorded animation/video of the visualization with no audio.
Importantly, the narration must be live. The demo video option is highly recommended because they are faster, you can still make eye contact with the audience while it plays, and there is little risk of technical hiccups.

### Grading
Here are several of the things we will be assessing when viewing your presentation:
#### Content & structure
- The introduction is attention-getting, lays out the talk well, and establishes a framework for the rest of the presentation.
- The presentation lays out a clear narrative that makes sense and flows.
- Material included is relevant to the overall message/purpose.
- Appropriate amount of material is prepared, and points made reflect well their relative importance.
- There is an obvious conclusion summarizing the presentation.

#### Presentation Advice

Acknowledging that students may all have varying levels of comfort with their presentation skills, below are some suggestions on doing your presentations:

- If possible, speakers should maintain good eye contact with the audience and are appropriately animated (e.g., gestures, moving around, etc.).
- If possible, speakers should use a clear, audible voice.
- Delivery is clearly practiced, poised, controlled, and smooth.
- Visualizations are well-prepared, informative, effective, and not distracting.
- Length of presentation is within the assigned time limits. Points are based on main presentation time.


```{tip}
If a group or a student within the group has a phobia of public speaking, the group (or the individual student) can arrange to do a private presentation with the instructor during scheduled student hours, or at another mutually agreed upon time before the end of the course term.
```

### Demo Reel

The demo reel should be a single video that is roughly 10 minutes long (+/- 1 min).
The recommended split is:

- 1 minute for an introduction and motivation for your dataset
- 2 minutes for each group member describing a single view and describing how it can be used to answer their research question.

Groups may choose to do their individual demos separately with a screen recording and stitch it together *OR* meet up virtually or in-person to record the demo reel together.

Ideally, you will submit a high-quality, high-resolution video that is uploaded **as an unlisted video** to a cloud service like Vimeo (preferred) or YouTube. If necessary, students can also upload their video file to iCloud Drive, Box.com, Google Drive, or OneDrive.

```{warning}
Note that students need not make their videos public (though you are welcome to if you like). Just make sure it's not set to private so the TAs and instructors can view the page.
```
